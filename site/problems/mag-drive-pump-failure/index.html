Why mag-drive pumps fail: dry running, decoupling and solids

Short answer

Magnetic drive pumps have no shaft seal to leak, but they rely on the pumped liquid to cool and lubricate their internal bushings. Most mag-drive failures come from running dry, running at too little flow, decoupling the magnets under excess torque, or passing solids through close internal clearances. Most are preventable with a power monitor and correct sizing for the liquid's specific gravity and viscosity.

Dry running

The bushings and thrust surfaces need liquid from the first second of operation. An empty suction tank, a closed suction valve or a lost prime can ruin them quickly. This is the most common mag-drive failure and the easiest to prevent.

Decoupling

The magnets can only transmit so much torque. Ask for more and the inner magnet stops following the outer one: the motor spins, the impeller does not. It happens when the liquid is heavier or more viscous than the pump was sized for, on cold starts with thick liquid, or when solids jam the impeller. Stop the pump and restart it; running decoupled generates heat and can weaken the magnets.

Too little flow

A small flow of liquid circulates behind the impeller to cool the drive. At very low flow that liquid heats up and can flash to vapor, which then behaves like dry running. Observe the manufacturer's minimum flow.

Solids

Internal clearances are small. Abrasive particles wear the bushings and shaft, and ferrous particles collect on the magnets. Strain the suction, or choose a design made to pass solids.

Protecting the pump

  • Fit a power monitor. It reads motor power, which drops sharply when the pump runs dry or decouples, and trips the motor.
  • Size the magnets for the real specific gravity and viscosity, including cold starts.
  • Match the lining to the chemical: ETFE-lined and PFA-lined pumps cover most corrosive service, and gear pumps cover metering duties.

Frequently asked questions

Can a mag-drive pump run dry at all?

Some designs tolerate a brief dry start better than others, depending on bushing material. None should be run dry as a matter of practice. Protect the pump rather than rely on tolerance.

How do I know the magnets have decoupled?

The motor runs but flow and pressure drop to nothing, and motor load falls well below normal. Stop the pump, find the reason, and restart.

When should I choose a mag-drive pump over a sealed pump?

When a leak is unacceptable: toxic, corrosive, expensive or regulated liquids. For dirty or solids-laden liquids a sealed pump with the right seal is often the better choice.
