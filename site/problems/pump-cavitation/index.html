Pump cavitation: signs, causes and how to stop it

Short answer

Cavitation happens when pressure at the pump inlet falls below the liquid's vapor pressure. Vapor bubbles form, then collapse violently inside the impeller. It sounds like gravel passing through the pump, it erodes the impeller, and the vibration shortens seal and bearing life. The fix is on the suction side: raise the net positive suction head available (NPSHa) or reduce what the pump requires (NPSHr).

What causes it

  • Suction tank level too low, or the pump lifting from too far below.
  • A clogged suction strainer or a partly closed suction valve.
  • Suction pipe that is too long, too small or has too many fittings.
  • Liquid that is hotter than the design assumed. Vapor pressure rises with temperature.
  • Running too far right on the curve. NPSHr climbs as flow rises.
  • A speed increase without rechecking suction conditions.

NPSH in one paragraph

NPSHa is the pressure pushing liquid into the pump, expressed as head: the absolute pressure on the liquid surface, plus the height of the liquid above the pump centerline (minus if the pump lifts), minus friction losses in the suction line, minus the liquid's vapor pressure. It has to exceed the NPSHr on the pump curve with margin. The NPSHr printed on most curves is the point where the pump has already lost 3% of its head, so a pump running exactly at NPSHr is already cavitating.

How to fix it

  • Raise the liquid level or lower the pump.
  • Clean the suction strainer and open suction valves fully.
  • Never throttle the suction valve to control flow. Control on the discharge.
  • Shorten or enlarge the suction pipe, and give the pump a straight run into the inlet.
  • Cool the liquid if the process allows.
  • Move the operating point left by throttling the discharge or slowing the pump with a variable frequency drive.
  • If the system cannot change, select a pump with a lower NPSHr.

When the right fix is not obvious, a hydraulic simulation of the system shows which change buys the most margin before anything is cut or bought.

Cavitation or air?

Entrained air makes a similar noise and the same unstable gauge readings. Air gets in through a vortex in a shallow tank, through packing or flange joints on a suction line under vacuum, or from a return line that discharges above the liquid surface. Air problems do not usually pit the impeller, and they often change when the tank level changes.

Frequently asked questions

How quickly does cavitation damage a pump?

Severe cavitation can pit an impeller within weeks. Mild cavitation may run for years while steadily shortening seal and bearing life. Either way the damage accumulates.

Does a variable frequency drive fix cavitation?

Slowing the pump reduces both flow and NPSHr, which often stops it. Speeding the pump up makes cavitation worse.

How much NPSH margin do I need?

There is no universal number. It depends on the pump type and how critical the service is. Common rules of thumb are a few feet above NPSHr, or a ratio of NPSHa to NPSHr comfortably above 1.1, with more margin for high-energy pumps. The Hydraulic Institute publishes guidance.
