How to calculate pump MTBF and find your bad actors

Short answer

Pump MTBF (mean time between failures) is the number of pumps in service multiplied by the time period, divided by the number of failures in that period. A plant with 400 pumps and 160 failures in 12 months has an MTBF of 30 months. The plant-wide number shows the trend. The per-pump numbers show which few machines, the bad actors, are consuming most of the repair budget.

Worked example

InputValue
Pumps in service400
Period12 months
Failures in the period160
MTBF = (400 x 12) / 16030 months

Decide what counts as a failure

The number only means something if a failure is defined the same way every time. A workable definition: any unplanned event that needs the pump opened or removed, including a seal change. Planned preventive work does not count. Write the definition down and keep to it.

Calculate it per pump as well as for the plant

The plant-wide figure hides the detail. One pump that has failed five times in 18 months and one that has run for six years both disappear into the same average. Per-pump history is where the decisions are.

Find the bad actors

  1. List every pump with its failures over the last two to three years.
  2. Add the cost of each failure: repair parts, labor and downtime.
  3. Rank by total cost, then by number of failures.
  4. The same few tags will sit at the top of both lists. Those are the bad actors.

In most plants a small share of the pumps accounts for a large share of the failures and the spend. That is good news: fixing a handful of machines moves the plant-wide number.

What to do with the list

Each bad actor gets a root cause analysis, not another repair. The causes repeat: running off the curve, seal environment, lubrication, suction conditions. Fix the cause, then watch the pump's own MTBF to confirm it worked.

The data you need

  • A complete, accurate equipment list.
  • Every repair with its date, what was found and what was replaced.
  • Parts and labor cost per repair.
  • An estimate of downtime cost per pump.

Most plants have this spread across work orders, purchase orders and people's memories. Southern Design's Reliability Management System builds the database, reports MTBF and cost per machine, and tracks each root cause from failure to fix.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good MTBF for pumps?

It varies widely with the industry, the type of pump and how failure is defined, so comparisons between plants are unreliable. The useful comparison is your own number against last year's.

How much history do I need?

At least 12 months to get a number, and two to three years to identify bad actors with confidence.

What is the difference between MTBF and MTTR?

MTBF measures how often equipment fails. MTTR, mean time to repair, measures how long it takes to get it running again. Reliability work raises the first; maintenance planning lowers the second.
