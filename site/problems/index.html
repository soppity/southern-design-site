Pump problems, and what fixes them

Plain answers to the failures that cost plants the most, written by the people who repair the equipment.

  • Why does my mechanical seal keep failing?A pump seal that fails again and again is reacting to dry running, a failed flush, shaft movement or the wrong materials. How to read the seal and fix it.
  • Pump cavitation: signs, causes and how to stop itCavitation sounds like gravel in the pump and destroys impellers, seals and bearings. The signs, the causes on the suction side, and the fixes that work.
  • Is your pump running off its curve? How to tell and what to doA pump run far from its best efficiency point bends its shaft and wears out seals and bearings. How to find the operating point and bring it back.
  • Why pump bearings fail early, and how to make them lastPump bearings rarely wear out. They fail from contaminated or wrong lubricant and from loads they were not chosen for. What the bearing and the oil tell you.
  • How to calculate pump MTBF and find your bad actorsPump MTBF is pumps in service times months, divided by failures. A worked example, what counts as a failure, and how to find the few pumps eating the budget.
  • Water hammer and pulsation in pump systems: causes and fixesBanging pipes, swinging gauges and broken supports come from water hammer or pump pulsation. What causes each and how dampeners, valves and drives fix them.
  • Why mag-drive pumps fail: dry running, decoupling and solidsMagnetic drive pumps have no seal to leak, but they fail from dry running, low flow, decoupling and solids. How to recognize each and protect the pump.
  • Should you repair or replace an industrial pump?Repair a pump that suits its duty and has a fixable cause. Replace one that is wrong for the job or costs too much to keep. Five questions that decide it.
